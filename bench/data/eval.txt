The central processing unit of a computer executes instructions that make up a program. It performs basic arithmetic, logic, controlling, and input and output operations specified by the instructions. Most modern processors are microprocessors, where the processor is contained on a single integrated circuit chip. A processor reads data from memory, but main memory is slow compared with the speed of the processor, so small and fast caches keep copies of recently used data close to the cores. When the data a program needs is already in the cache, the processor can continue without waiting; when it is not, the processor stalls until the data arrives from main memory.

The water cycle describes how water moves continuously on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. Water evaporates from oceans, lakes, and rivers when it is heated by the sun. The vapor rises into the atmosphere, cools, and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. When the droplets combine and grow heavy enough, they fall back to the ground as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Some of this water flows over the land into streams and rivers, some soaks into the soil and becomes groundwater, and some is taken up by plants and released again through their leaves.

The printing press was developed in Europe in the fifteenth century. Before it, books were copied by hand, which was slow and expensive, so few people owned them. Movable metal type allowed pages to be composed quickly and printed many times, and the cost of books fell sharply. Printed pamphlets, books, and newspapers spread new ideas across the continent within a few years, which changed religion, science, politics, and education. Literacy rose as reading material became cheaper and more widely available.

Learning a new programming language is easier when you build small projects instead of only reading about the language. Start with the basic syntax, then write short programs that read input, process it, and print a result. Read code written by experienced developers, and pay attention to the idioms and the standard library. Use the debugger to step through programs and understand how they behave. Most importantly, practice regularly, because skills grow through repetition and through fixing your own mistakes.
